# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance of timely public reporting for readers who follow the global intelligence industry: agencies and oversight, OSINT, cyber threat intelligence, commercial intelligence and investigations firms, and geospatial or satellite intelligence.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Groundbreaking.** A major change in intelligence authorities or leadership, a significant official disclosure, a widely consequential threat-intelligence finding, or a market-moving development at a major intelligence-industry company.
- **7-8: High value.** Important agency, policy, OSINT, threat-intelligence, commercial, or GEOINT developments that a professional audience should see promptly, supported by concrete facts from a reputable source.
- **5-6: Interesting.** Incremental but relevant updates, narrower investigations, secondary official guidance, or useful industry analysis that is worth knowing but not urgent.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Routine notices, thinly sourced items, event or award announcements, or vendor posts that are mostly promotional.
- **0-2: Noise.** Spam, off-topic material, generic developer or AI news, routine vulnerability lists, stock-touting pieces, or geopolitical commentary without a specific intelligence-industry hook.

# Evaluation guidance

Prefer primary official sources and established reporting over commentary. Score an item only on what the supplied source actually establishes. Clearly treat allegations, anonymous claims, and unconfirmed media reports as unverified; do not reward sensational headlines. Downrank general war, diplomacy, or political coverage unless the item itself is about intelligence organizations, methods, oversight, industry capabilities, or GEOINT. Downrank generic cybersecurity product news and isolated low-impact data breaches with no intelligence-industry significance. Source quality and specificity matter more than popularity.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
